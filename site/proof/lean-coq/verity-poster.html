The 60-second version Three quantum spins generate a space with 125 dimensions. A symmetry operator divides that space into sectors. A polynomial selects sectors totalling exactly 47 dimensions. Ordinary mathematics explains why this works; Python checks it with exact arithmetic. Lean and Coq let the reasoning itself be translated into a form a computer can rigorously examine. Parts of that formal verification have been developed and reportedly accepted — the complete concrete-matrix proof still needs a confirmed compiler-accepted certificate. The achievement is not finding the number 47. It is building a reproducible path from calculation to formal proof.
01 The Two ExaminersWho — or what — decides a proof is correct?

Imagine a mathematician writes a proof on a blackboard. Other mathematicians read it, check each step, and decide whether the conclusion follows.

Now imagine a second kind of examiner — one that cannot be persuaded by reputation, eloquence, impressive equations, or beautiful diagrams. It accepts a proof only when every required logical step is justified under explicitly stated rules.

👤

Conventional Proof

A human mathematician writes an argument. Other humans examine its correctness.

Standard: mathematical reasoning and expert scrutiny.
🤖

Machine-Checked Proof

A formal statement and proof are submitted to Lean or Coq. The computer checks whether the proof follows from the encoded definitions, axioms, and previously accepted results.

Standard: proof accepted by a formal kernel.
Both are legitimate Machine checking adds a reproducible layer of scrutiny. It does not automatically make a theorem important, physically true, or correctly applicable to the world.
02 What Lean and Coq Actually AreNot app-building languages — verification engines
🔧
Proof assistants Programming environments in which mathematical statements can be expressed precisely and their proofs checked by a small logical verification engine. They are not ordinary programming languages used primarily to build websites or applications.
Lean Coq / Rocq
Used by Mathematicians, formal-methods researchers, computer scientists Mathematical logic, programming-language research, hardware & software verification
Known for Large mathematical formalizations and verified software Foundational logic, verified compilers, industrial verification
Note Lean 4 is the current generation Newer releases are known as Rocq

Where: universities · research laboratories · industrial verification work. Purpose: not to produce convincing-looking mathematics — but to make the logical justification inspectable and checkable.

04 The Headline TheoremWhat a successful formal proof would actually prove
dim ker (C − 6I)(C − 30I) = 47
E47 · the selected sector

A conventional proof explains why this follows from the representation’s spin decomposition.

A complete Lean or Coq proof of the concrete matrix theorem would establish that the conclusion follows from the formal definitions of the 125-dimensional operator and the proof assistant’s accepted logical foundations.

🔑
Stronger than PASS That is stronger than asking a computer to calculate 47 and print PASS. It means the proof checker has accepted a chain of reasoning that justifies the result.

08 The Central JuxtapositionSound vs. method vs. achievement vs. remainder
What sounds extraordinary
A computer can certify a theorem about a 125-dimensional quantum mathematical construction.
What is actually extraordinary
The computer need not trust the mathematician’s authority — it checks the proof against explicit rules.
What has been achieved here
An exact, structured E47 construction with reproducible computation and a substantial formal-verification program.
What remains
A complete, compiler-accepted Lean and Coq certificate for the concrete operator and its desired properties.

Notice: discarding 78 of 125 costs 1.41 bits. Discarding 124 of 125 — almost everything — costs only 6.97. Five times the wreckage, five times the price? No. The cost is the logarithm of a ratio, not a tally of the dead.

Which means something strange and rather beautiful: the price doesn't know anything about Nick's construction except that it keeps 47 out of 125. Not the spectral gap. Not the operator. Nothing. Thermodynamics looks at the whole apparatus and charges only for the narrowing.

So what

Why anyone should care

Every time anything anywhere narrows many possibilities down to fewer — a memory chip resetting, a cell committing to a fate, a mind settling an ambiguity — the universe sends a bill, and the bill is kT ln2 per bit. It was worked out by Rolf Landauer in 1961 and measured in a lab in 2012 on a single microscopic bead.

It is one of the very few places where information, a thing that feels abstract and weightless, turns out to have a price tag denominated in joules — and therefore, through E = mc², in kilograms.

Not because thoughts weigh something. Because forgetting is work.
